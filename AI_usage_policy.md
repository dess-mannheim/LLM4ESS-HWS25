### About using AI language tools / LLMs for your projects
 
As a course dedicated to the use of LLMs in social research, we are aware of the popularity and capabilities of these models. Nevertheless, when conducting your course project, we urge that you are very careful with using such tools. This is both so that you can make the most of this opportunity to develop your own skills and competencies and to avoid potential plagiarism issues. 

From this course, you will gain experience of collaborating as a team to develop a piece of research. The projects gives you the opportunity to design and implement a research project which helps you to understand human or LLM behaviour. If you allow LLMs to take over key aspects of the project, then you will limit your own learning opportunities. Additionally, LLMs can present a risk to the academic integrity of your work. Never rely on LLMs as your primary source of information or put LLM output directly into your report. 

At the same time, there are many useful ways to adopt LLMs as a tool, for example to supplement your research of the topic, gain a better understanding of the domain of interest, identify key researchers and publications, form new research ideas and questions, improve the style of your writing, understand technical concepts or explain code and algorithms. All of these still require your human input and involvement, since you will be fully responsible for the end submission.

We have now had multiple cases at our chair of students submitting text with significant errors or mistakes likely introduced by LLMs. This resulted in extensive discussions  at the chair and at our faculty about whether the students would have to fail their course, or whether passing with a very bad grade would still be possible. Using LLMs without sufficient human intervention puts your team project, and thus your graduate education, at risk.
To clarify the situation, when you hand in your report you must include a declaration at the end stating that you have written the whole report on your own, i.e., without any external help. Thus, when we receive your report, we will have to assume that all text has been produced by you, and as a consequence you will be fully accountable for what is in your report. In that context, there are many ways how text produced by LLMs can negatively affect your report, for instance:

- These models are prone to hallucinating false information including false references, even when simply rephrasing paragraphs. In the case that some of such hallucinations end up in your report, the consequences for your grade can be very serious, including failing your team project or the entire course. It is your responsibility to adhere to the standards of good academic practice and writing.
- These models tend to use very flowery, non-scientific language, which is easy to detect and will make your report look bad too. If, for example, your introduction describes how you “embarked on a scientific journey,” or that you “delve into the intricacies of your meticulous methodology” in later chapters, this will negatively influence your grade.
- Text generated or rephrased by LLMs will in all likelihood differ from your writing style, reducing the quality of the report. You might also consider the possibility of new LLM detection techniques that have not been invented yet but might be available 10 years from now to check your report for undue use of such tools.
- Do not make LLMs generate content for your from scratch --- writing is an essential ingredient for clarifying your thinking and as such by offloading it to LLMs fully, you're dulling your own skills.

Practically speaking, feel free to use LLMs in an ethical manner while taking full accountability for the contents of your report, code, and presentations. Any mistakes found in them will be your responsibility. In the section where you report the contribution of each team member, you should also include a statement about which tasks you used LLMs for.
