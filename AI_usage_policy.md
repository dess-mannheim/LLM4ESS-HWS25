### About using AI language tools / LLMs for your projects
 
As a course dedicated to the use of LLMs in social research, we are aware of the popularity and the capabilities of these models, but at the same time, when conducting your course project, we urge that you are very careful with using such tools. 
 
Never rely on LLMs as your primary source of information. Do not put LLM output directly in your report. At the same time, there are still many useful ways to adopt LLMs as a tool, for example to supplement your research of the topic, gain a better understanding of the domain of interest, identify key researchers and publications, form new research ideas and questions, improve the style of your writing, understand technical concepts or explain code and algorithms.

We have now had two cases of students submitting text with significant errors or mistakes likely introduced by ChatGPT. This resulted in extensive discussions  at the chair and at our faculty whether the students would have to fail their course, or whether passing with a very bad grade would still be possible. Using LLMs to directly produce text puts your team project, and thus your graduate education, at risk.
To give you the bigger picture of this, when you hand in your report, you must include a declaration after the conclusion stating that you have written the whole report on your own, i.e., without any external help. Thus, when we receive your report, we will have to assume that all text has been produced by yourself, and in consequence you will be accountable for what is in your report. In that context, there are many ways how text produced by LLMs can negatively affect your report, for instance:

- These models are prone to hallucinating false information including false references, even when simply rephrasing paragraphs. In the case that some of such hallucinations end up in your report, the consequences for your grade can be very serious, including failing your team project or the entire course. It is your responsibility to adhere to the standards of good academic practice and writing.
- These models tend to use very flowery, non-scientific language, which is easy to detect and will make your report look bad too. If, for example, your introduction describes how you “embarked on a scientific journey,” or that you “delve into the intricacies of your meticulous methodology” in later chapters, this will negatively influence your grade.
- In general, text generated or rephrased by LLMs will in all likelihood differ from your writing style.  You might also consider the possibility of new LLM detection techniques that have not been invented yset but might be available 10 years from now to check your report for undue use of such tools.
- Ideally do not make LLMs generate content for your from scratch --- writing is an essential ingredient for clarifying your thinking and as such by offloading it to LLMs fully, you're dulling your own skills.

Practically speaking, feel free to use LLMs in an ethical manner while taking full accountability for the contents of your report, code, and presentations. Any mistakes found in them will be your responsibility. In the section where you include what each team member did, include a statement for which tasks you used LLMs.
